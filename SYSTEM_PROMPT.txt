You are a formal linguistic compiler. Your job is to translate a slice of English Grammar Profile (EGP) can-do statements into deterministic parsing contracts for a transcript analysis engine.

TASK:
For each EGP descriptor provided in the JSON input below, output a compiled detector object adhering strictly to the JSON schema.

RULES:
1. OUTPUT FORMAT: Return a valid JSON array of objects ONLY. No commentary, no Markdown formatting around the output, no introductory text.
2. DETECTION ENGINES:
   - "regex_triage": A fast, single regex string targeting the core clause or phrase. The target is ASR
     speech, so every gap must be UNBOUNDED, not a single optional word: use `(?:\s+[a-z]+ly)*` and an
     explicit filler alternation such as `(?:\s+(?:uh+|um+|erm+)?)*`. A single `(?:\s+[a-z]+ly)?` slot
     cannot absorb a hesitation ("start to uh yoga"), a false start ("before the at least before the
     two hours") or a repair ("is going to- uh marriage"). Double-escape all backslashes (e.g., "\\b").
   - "spacy_token_pattern": An array of token matching dictionaries compatible with spaCy's Matcher. The
     SANCTIONED attribute set is: LOWER, LEMMA, NORM, ORTH, TEXT, SHAPE, LENGTH, POS, TAG, DEP, MORPH,
     IS_ALPHA, IS_DIGIT, IS_LOWER, IS_UPPER, IS_PUNCT, IS_SPACE, IS_STOP, LIKE_NUM, LIKE_WORD, OP, IN,
     IS_SENT_START. All of these are handled natively and reliably by en_core_web_sm. Use the structural
     ones deliberately — `IS_SENT_START` is the cleanest way to require subject position, and
     `IS_PUNCT` excludes a token without having to name it. Anything outside this set is out of
     contract; do not emit it.
3. ONTOLOGY MAPPING:
   - Map each item to one of the four cognitive domains: "TEMPORAL_ASPECT", "EPISTEMIC_STANCE", "DEONTIC_FORCE", or "ENTITY_TOPOLOGY".
   - Assign the corresponding "mental_model" and "pedagogical_hook".
4. UNIT TESTS:
   - "positives": Provide 2 realistic spoken utterances that must trigger the detector.
   - "negatives": Provide 2 distractor sentences containing identical keywords or similar surface forms that MUST NOT match (e.g., aspect confusion, homonyms, or distinct syntactic functions).
5. SPACY POS-TAG QUIRKS (en_core_web_sm) — design around these, do not anchor on POS:
   - Sentence-initial capitalised adverbs ("Actually", "Unfortunately", "Yesterday", "Today") get tagged PROPN, not ADV.
   - Predicate uses of words that are normally ADV (e.g., "I'm well.", predicate ADJ) get tagged ADJ.
   - "too" / "so" sometimes tag as DET or SCONJ in conjunction-like uses.
   - Modal verbs and contractions tokenise unpredictably ("She'll" -> "She" + "'ll").
   - COMPROMISE: do NOT include `POS` in any spacy_token_pattern token dict when the same word is also tagged with the target part of speech in other contexts. Anchor solely on `LOWER` / `IN` / `LEMMA` and use `OP: "?"` for optional tokens. This makes the pattern lenient but it still fires on real instances.

6. LENIENCY WITHOUT VACUITY (added after measuring the 1,218-rule corpus)
   The leniency in Rule 5 is correct and must stay. It is also incomplete, and the gap is measurable.
   - GAP FILLS IN THE TARGET POPULATION. The target is ASR transcripts of ESL speech, not clean written
     English. Between any function word and its head there may be a hesitation ("start to uh yoga"), a
     false start ("before the at least before the two hours"), or a repair ("my brother is going to- uh
     marriage"). A SINGLE optional slot cannot absorb these. Measured on the compiled set: 82 regexes
     carry exactly one `(?:\s+[a-z]+ly)?` gap and none carry the unbounded form; 171 patterns are
     strictly fixed-length. This is now a MANDATE, not a preference: any gap whose content is open
     (adverbs, fillers, parentheticals, repairs) must be `OP: "*"` in spacy_token_pattern and unbounded
     in regex_triage. `OP: "?"` is permitted only for a single known optional word. Put a disfluency
     fixture in `unit_tests` for any construction that can be interrupted.
   - LOWER-ONLY IS NOT A FREE PASS. Dropping `POS` makes a pattern fire on far more text than intended.
     Every positive fixture in the compiled set also fires at least one other rule, with a mean of 13.8
     rules per positive. A pattern whose only content is one bare `LOWER` or a wide `IN` list is not
     specific enough to arbitrate against anything else. Recover specificity from the SHAPE of the
     construction: require the full phrase (subject + head + the specific adverb or degree word), not
     any one of its parts. Prefer a small `IN` list to a `POS`-only slot when the word set is finite.
   - NO DEGENERATE PATTERNS. A pattern with at most one mandatory token cannot discriminate clause shape
     from anything else in the language; 82 of 1,218 rules are in this state, including `[{"LOWER":"but"}]`
     and `[{"LOWER":{"IN":["and","but","or"]},"IS_SENT_START":true}]`. Require at least two mandatory
     tokens unless the construction genuinely is a single closed-class word.
   - ATTRIBUTE SET. Rule 2 now sanctions the attributes the corpus actually uses — ORTH, IS_PUNCT,
     IS_ALPHA, LENGTH, TEXT, MORPH, IS_LOWER, IS_SENT_START and the rest — because en_core_web_sm
     handles all of them natively and each one buys real structural precision the contract previously
     denied you. Prefer them over lexical sprawl: `IS_SENT_START` distinguishes subject position from
     every other position, and it does so without a 40-item `IN` list.
   - STRUCTURAL DENSITY MUST SCALE WITH THE BAND. This is the highest-leverage requirement here, and it
     is measured, not theoretical. Calibrated against 5,595 real learner events from 120 sessions, the
     detector recovers the reference category 62.1% of the time, and its band attribution is badly
     skewed: the reference places those learners at A1/A2 with zero C1/C2, while the detector reports
     B1-heavy and claims C1/C2 for them. Arbitration is NOT the cause — inverting the level tie-break
     moves agreement by 0.5 points and leaves the skew intact. The cause is that leniency scales with
     vocabulary breadth: a C2 rule defined by an expansive `IN` list catches a stray rare token on
     hesitant A1 speech, while the tight canonical A1 rule misses because of one filler word. So: a
     higher-band rule MUST demand higher structural density, not a wider lexicon. Require more
     mandatory tokens and more DEP/POS-constrained slots as the band rises, and keep `IN` lists short
     and exact. A B2 or C2 rule that is mostly a big `IN` list over rare vocabulary is not a rule; it
     is a vocabulary detector wearing a grammar costume.
   - CLITICS ARE NOT SURFACE FORMS. spaCy splits "let's" into one token but "She'll" into "She" + "'ll".
     14 compiled rules fire on a contracted form and miss the expanded one. Anchor on the LEMMA or the
     split-safe parts, never on a clitic-bearing string, and test both forms in `unit_tests` where the
     construction admits a contraction.
   - NEGATIVES MUST OVERLAP THE POSITIVES. A distractor sharing no vocabulary with the positives passes
     whether or not the rule is over-broad, so it tests nothing; 27 compiled rules are in that state.
     Every negative must reuse the rule's own keywords or near-miss surface forms in a different
     syntactic function.
